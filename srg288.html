Strongly regular graphs · open case from Brouwer's table

There is no strongly regular graph with parameters (288, 105, 52, 30)

No graph on 288 vertices, each of degree 105, where adjacent vertices share 52 neighbours and non-adjacent vertices share 30. Its complement would be the point graph of a partial geometry pg(7,25,5), so that geometry does not exist either. The vertices become vectors of an even lattice of rank 28. Gluing into an even unimodular lattice of rank 32 turns its theta series into 1,297 exact linear equations, and an exact certificate shows that they have no solution. No symmetry of the graph is assumed, and no classification of lattices is needed.

Step 1 · spectrum

From eigenvalues to vectors

The adjacency matrix A has eigenvalues 105, 25 and −3. The matrix G = A + 3I + J sends the eigenvalue −3 to 0, so G is positive semidefinite of rank 28 and is the Gram matrix of 288 vectors in 28 dimensions.

eigenvalue of A105multiplicity 1  → G: 396
eigenvalue of A25multiplicity 27  → G: 28
eigenvalue of A−3multiplicity 260  → G: 0

Each vertex becomes a vector of norm 4; adjacent vertices have inner product 2, non-adjacent ones 1. They span an even lattice L of rank 28. We fix a maximal even overlattice N of L, the host; its discriminant form is anisotropic.

Step 8 · Table 3 of the paper

Four genera, four certificates

Each certificate is a vector λ of integer multipliers, one per equation. The combination λᵀA is nonnegative on every type count, so λᵀb bounds the counts. The bar shows the forced value against what a host would need.

The largest multipliers have 41, 33, 24 and 118 decimal digits, so the certificates are not printed in the paper; they are in the Zenodo record together with the checker.

Controls

The same machinery lets real graphs through

No known graph has a host of the target shape. The controls use Latin-square graphs of order 10, srg(100,27,10,6), whose matrix A + 3I + J also has rank 28 with norms 4 and inner products 2 and 1; their true hosts have determinant 1300. A control cannot prove the theorem; it shows that the mechanism recovers configurations that exist.

Verification

How the result was checked

The result was reached three times, by different routes that share no code. The route of the paper (the second derivation) uses one complement per genus and harmonic degrees up to 10; the earlier routes needed odd unimodular lattices of rank 29 and 31 or many complements coupled together.

The first derivation and the audit were done by separate Claude sessions; the second derivation, also by Claude, found the certificates of the paper. Codex (OpenAI's coding agent) reviewed them, rebuilt the certificate functional, wrote the standalone checker and the solver-free admissibility check, and built the true-host controls. The referee's verdict was “accept with minor revisions”, with no fatal or major finding. The exact checker and its thirteen input files are in the Zenodo record. The grade is computer-assisted: the four certificates cannot be checked by hand, and the 493 forbidden entries of the admissibility table have no hand certificate. No human expert has read the paper yet.
