Strongly regular graphs · open case from Brouwer's table

There is no strongly regular graph with parameters (154, 72, 26, 40)

No graph on 154 vertices, each of degree 72, where adjacent vertices share 26 neighbours and non-adjacent vertices share 40. The proof turns the graph into vectors of a lattice and shows that no lattice can hold them. Almost every step is a short hand argument.

Step 1 · spectrum

From eigenvalues to vectors

The adjacency matrix A has three eigenvalues. The matrix G = −A + 2I + 2J turns the eigenvalue 2 into 0, so G is positive semidefinite of rank 22 and is the Gram matrix of 154 vectors in 22 dimensions.

eigenvalue of A72multiplicity 1  → G: 238
eigenvalue of A2multiplicity 132  → G: 0
eigenvalue of A−16multiplicity 21  → G: 18

Each vertex becomes a vector of norm 4; adjacent vertices have inner product 1, non-adjacent ones 2. Their integer span is an even lattice of rank 1 + 21 = 22.

Formal verification

The arithmetic core is checked by Lean

The core of the proof is formalized in the Lean 4 proof assistant with the Mathlib library. Lean's kernel checks every step, so these parts do not have to be trusted on the strength of programs or of any reader. Project: github.com/tonykoval/srg154-lean (builds in about a minute after downloading the Mathlib cache).

No sorry, no native_decide, no added axioms; only Lean's standard axioms propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound. Two independent reviews rebuilt the project from scratch (76 audited theorems) and confirmed that the formal statements match the paper and are not vacuous. This is a conditional formalization of the core, not a full formal proof from the graph.
