Strongly regular graphs · constructions

New graphs for eighteen open parameter sets

For eighteen parameter sets marked “?” in Brouwer's table we give explicit strongly regular graphs, in three papers. The first nine sets, with 262 graphs in all, pairwise non-isomorphic within each set, are now marked as realised in the table, on the basis of this work. The second paper adds five sets on 306, 400, 576, 784 and 1296 vertices, with at least 24 graphs in version 1.0 of the paper and at least 62 among the graphs we hold now (class counts and subgroup lifts of 17–18 Oct 2026; local draft v1.1 of the paper, not yet deposited); Brouwer's table marks them “+” since 11 October 2026. The third paper adds four sets on 160, 232, 261 and 280 vertices, with eight graphs in version 1.0 of the paper and at least 64 among the graphs we hold now (class counts and subgroup lifts of 17–18 Oct 2026; local draft v1.1 of the paper, not yet deposited); these rows were still “?” in the table on 12 October 2026.

Bush-type Hadamard graphs with two spreads deleted

A symmetric Bush-type Hadamard matrix K of order n² whose n × n blocks have unit diagonal, for instance one of Kharaghani's matrices, defines a strongly regular graph Γ₀ = ½(J + K) − I with the parameters of the Latin square graph LSn/2+1(n). Its block rows and its block columns are two orthogonal spreads of Delsarte cliques. Deleting one spread gives the known graphs with λ = μ of Bonato, Holzmann and Kharaghani (2001); by an elementary spread lemma (essentially Haemers and Tonchev, 1996), deleting both gives a strongly regular graph with the parameters of LSn/2−1(n):

srg( n², (n−1)(n−2)/2, (n/2−1)(n/2−2) + 2, (n/2−1)(n/2−2) ), eigenvalues 1 ± n/2

Kharaghani's matrices (1985) exist for every Hadamard order n, so such a graph exists for every n = 4m ≥ 8. For n = 20, 24, 28, 36 these are the last four rows above; beyond the table, n = 40 and 44 give srg(1600,741,344,342) and srg(1936,903,422,420). A second source, found first from the search hits, is the rule (a,b) ~ (c,d) iff a ≠ c, b ≠ d and HabHbcHcdHda = 1, for a symmetric Hadamard matrix H of order n; it is an instance of the same theorem and also has a direct proof. The graphs come from Paley's matrices, the symplectic regular two-graph on 36 points, the Latin square graphs of ℤ₆ and S₃, and Kharaghani's matrices; pairwise non-isomorphism within each set is by nauty canonical forms. None of the graphs tested on these four vertex numbers is a Latin square graph (an exhaustive count of cliques of size n), so they say nothing about the orthogonal arrays OA(n, n/2 − 1) named in the table.

The graph on 306 vertices

Δ has as vertices the 300 non-square points of the parabolic quadric Q(4,5) and the 6 points of a totally singular line ℓ, which form a Delsarte clique. Two non-square points y, z are adjacent if B(y,z) = 0 and their perpendicular points on ℓ differ, or if z lies on the line through y and its perpendicular point; each non-square point is also adjacent to its perpendicular point on ℓ. Its automorphism group is the stabiliser of ℓ in SO(5,5), of order 60,000, with orbits 300 + 6. Δ has the parameters of the collinearity graph of a generalized quadrangle GQ(5,10), but only 61 cliques of size 6 where such a quadrangle would need 561 lines, so it is a sporadic, non-geometric pseudo-GQ(5,10). The same rule over 𝔽₃ gives the collinearity graph of Q(4,3); over 𝔽₇ and 𝔽₉ it is not strongly regular. Strong regularity of Δ is an exact computer check, by several programs; there is no hand proof yet.

Open: a third spread?

A third spread of Γ₀, orthogonal to the block rows and block columns, could be deleted as well and would give the parameters of LSn/2−2(n): (400,152,60,56), (576,230,94,90), (784,324,136,132) and (1296,560,244,240), all marked “?”. None was found. For n = 12 a SAT model over all symmetric Latin squares is unsatisfiable, so no Kharaghani matrix of order 144 has one; the runs for n = 20 stopped after 7200 seconds without a verdict, so these rows remain open.

The same search, extended to three orbits and to direct products G × Cm, found five graphs on four rows of the table; enumerating all fusions of the same group actions, instead of stopping at the first, gave three more. None of the eight graphs is vertex-transitive, so no search over transitive actions could have found them.

Five graphs over the projective line

Let 𝒫 be the 9 points of PG(1,8), ℒ the 36 pairs of points of 𝒫, and ℰ the 28 conjugate pairs {e, e⁸} of points of PG(1,64) ∖ PG(1,8). The vertices are labelled pairs or labelled conjugate pairs, each carrying a label in 𝔽₈*, together with a small orbit: the 9 points (261), the 28 conjugate pairs (280), or the 36 pairs (232). Adjacency is decided by cross ratios: for two labelled vertices on disjoint pairs, the quotient (rule Q) or the product (rule P) of the two labels must equal the cross ratio of the two pairs or its inverse; for pairs sharing a point it must equal 1; a labelled pair and a conjugate pair are adjacent when their cross ratio is a primitive cube root of unity.

GL(2,8) ⋊ C₆ = (SL(2,8) × C₇) ⋊ (C₃ × C₂), the C₃ generated by the Frobenius map and the C₂ inverting the labels. In both graphs on 261 vertices the 9 points form a Delsarte clique; in both graphs on 280 vertices the 28 conjugate pairs form a Hoffman coclique; the local graph of a point of ΓQ261 is the 8 × 8 rook's graph.

Three graphs on 160 vertices

Γ(1)160 lives on the 16 cells of a 4 × 4 grid and three copies of the 48 flags of its even transversals; its automorphism group is C₃ × 2⁴:(S₃ × S₃), of order 1,728, with orbits 144 + 16, and the cells form a Hoffman coclique. Γ(2)160 and Γ(3)160 are fusions of orbitals of 2⁴:(S₃ × S₃) acting on 96 + 48 + 16 points, with automorphism groups C₃:(2⁴:(S₃ × S₃)) of order 1,728 (not isomorphic to that of Γ(1)160) and 2⁴:(S₃ × S₃) of order 576.

Not geometric, and sporadic

The four rows are the parameters of the partial geometries pg(9,5,3), pg(7,8,2), pg(8,7,2) and pg(9,6,2). None of the eight graphs is geometric: the three graphs on 160 vertices and both on 280 have no Delsarte clique at all; the graphs on 261 vertices have only 46 and 100 Delsarte cliques, fewer than the 232 lines of a pg(8,7,2); and no subfamily of the 513 Delsarte cliques of Γ232 partitions its edge set. The same rules over PG(1,q) give, for q = 4, the classical skew-lines graph of PG(3,2) and a second known srg(35,16,6,8), and no strongly regular graph for q = 16 or q = 32 under the groups searched (complete solver verdicts): within this model the graphs for q = 8 are sporadic.

Also in data/: the verifier verify_srg.py, the README.txt of the ancillary files and SHA256SUMS with the full hashes. SHA256SUMS also lists programs and connection sets that belong to the full archive but are not copied here.

python3 verify_srg.py srg324_136_58_56.g6 324 136 58 56

The verifier is plain Python 3 with no packages. It decodes each line and checks A² = kI + λA + μ(J − I − A) directly: every vertex has degree k, and every pair of distinct vertices has λ (adjacent) or μ (non-adjacent) common neighbours. It passes on all 264 lines of the eleven files of the first paper and on the eight files of the third (python3 verify_srg.py G280b.g6 280 63 14 14, and so on). The two sum-graph files contain the graphs obtained from a known abelian (320,88,24) difference set of Davis and Jedwab (1997); they are not vertex-transitive (64 automorphisms) and are not counted among the new graphs.

Independent review was uneven, and the paper says exactly what it covered. The first drafts (graphs reported on 30 September) were refereed by separate Claude agents with their own code. Several later additions (from 1 and 2 October) were not refereed independently at the time. On 6 October a Codex referee re-verified strong regularity and pairwise non-isomorphism of all 256 graphs then listed, with its own program, without recomputing every invariant. The eight srg(324,114,36,42) nos. 15–22, added on 7 October 2026, have not been refereed independently. For the third paper every computer result was reproduced by a blind audit with separately written code (Claude Fable), and version 2 was refereed by a further session with its own code, which re-implemented the constructions from the text.
