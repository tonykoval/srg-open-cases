Strongly regular graphs · open case from Brouwer's table

There is no strongly regular graph with parameters (105, 52, 21, 30)

No graph on 105 vertices, each of degree 52, where adjacent vertices share 21 neighbours and non-adjacent vertices share 30. The vertices become vectors of an even lattice of rank 21. Gluing that lattice into a unimodular lattice of dimension 23 leaves finitely many hosts, and every one of them is excluded. No symmetry of the graph is assumed.

Step 1 · spectrum

From eigenvalues to vectors

The adjacency matrix A has eigenvalues 52, 2 and −11. The matrix G = −A + 2I + 2J sends the eigenvalue 2 to 0, so G is positive semidefinite of rank 21 and is the Gram matrix of 105 vectors in 21 dimensions.

eigenvalue of A52multiplicity 1  → G: 160
eigenvalue of A2multiplicity 84  → G: 0
eigenvalue of A−11multiplicity 20  → G: 13

Each vertex becomes a vector of norm 4; adjacent vertices have inner product 1, non-adjacent ones 2. They span an even lattice L of rank 21. For the complement, which has smallest eigenvalue −3, the same matrix reads Ā + 3I + J, the representation used for srg(69,20,7,5), srg(99,42,21,15) and srg(162,69,36,24).
