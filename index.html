Strongly regular graphs · September–October 2026

Open cases of Brouwer's table, settled

A. E. Brouwer's table lists every feasible parameter set of strongly regular graphs and marks with “?” those for which nobody knows whether a graph exists. In September and October 2026 we proved that nine of these graphs do not exist, one paper for each; showed with one uniform lattice condition that no graph exists for 70 further open complementary pairs; and constructed graphs for twenty further parameter sets, four of them (on 160, 232, 261 and 280 vertices) on 12 October 2026 and the latest, srg(217,66,15,22), on 18 October 2026. We also proved that the regular two-graphs on 126 and 176 points are unique, a question Brouwer and Van Maldeghem list as open; as a consequence four parameter sets of the table each have exactly one graph. By the same method, the Delsarte graph and the M₂₃ graph are the only graphs with their parameters, (243,110,37,60) and (253,112,36,60), with no group assumed; with the complements, four more sets have exactly one graph. All results are preprints with open code and data.

Of the open rows with at most 100 vertices, srg(85,42,20,21) is now settled by existence: H. Verma, arXiv:2610.08893, which we confirmed independently (details), and srg(96,35,10,14) is one of the 70 excluded pairs. Still open with v ≤ 100: srg(88,27,6,9), srg(99,14,1,2) (Conway's 99-graph problem) and srg(100,33,8,12). On the existing row srg(243,22,1,2) we added three graphs to the four known ones (seven graphs; note deposited 20 Oct 2026, doi:10.5281/zenodo.23491895).

Nonexistence · a uniform condition

Seventy more pairs, by one lattice condition

The lattice method of Bannai, Munemasa and Venkov for tight spherical designs, extended to every strongly regular graph with integral eigenvalues, gives a test on the parameters alone. A complete sweep of Brouwer's table finds 70 open complementary pairs that fail it. None of them overlaps the twenty-three results above.

Uniqueness

Parameter sets with exactly one graph

Brouwer and Van Maldeghem write, for 126 and for 176 points, that it is unknown whether the regular two-graph is unique. Both are unique. Their descendants give four parameter sets that Brouwer's table now marks “!” (unique). The same method shows that the srg(243,110,37,60) and the srg(253,112,36,60) are unique, where only uniqueness among rank-3 graphs was known; since 11 October 2026 Brouwer's table marks both “!”.

Methods

How the results were obtained

Nonexistence: the lattice method

  1. The eigenvalues of the graph give a positive semidefinite Gram matrix, so the vertices become short vectors spanning an integral lattice.
  2. The sum of all vertex vectors gives a special dual vector, and every dual vector satisfies exact counting identities. Many small dual norms become impossible.
  3. Milgram's formula leaves a few discriminant forms. Gluing turns each into a unimodular lattice: one from a known classification (Niemeier, Borcherds, Chenevier), or, for 288, 405 and 476, an even unimodular lattice of rank 32 or 40, or, for 196, odd unimodular lattices of rank 27 and 28 with their shadows whose harmonic theta series give exact linear equations.
  4. Each candidate host is excluded: by an explicit forbidden vector, by an identity proved by hand, by an exhaustive enumeration with exact certificates, or by an exact rational certificate that the theta-series equations have no admissible solution.

For the 70 pairs, the first steps alone suffice: no admissible discriminant form exists, so no gluing and no theta series are needed.

Existence: unions of orbitals

  1. Take a finite group acting transitively on v points. Any union of its orbitals (orbits on pairs) is a graph on which the group acts.
  2. Search the unions with the right degree for the strong regularity equation A² = kI + λA + μ(J − I − A), using the exact intersection numbers of the action.
  3. Sources: coset actions from the GAP table-of-marks library, affine groups over 𝔽₃⁴, descents to subgroups, and coset layers over p-groups.
  4. On 324 vertices, also exact searches for partial difference sets in abelian groups, using character values and a “slice” decomposition.
  5. Groups with two orbits, on G/H₁ ⊔ G/H₂. Its hits led to a construction by hand: a Bush-type Hadamard graph with two orthogonal spreads of Delsarte cliques deleted.
  6. Groups with three orbits, and direct products G × Cm. Its hits on 232, 261 and 280 vertices have short descriptions by cross ratios over PG(1,8) ⊂ PG(1,64); one graph on 160 vertices lives on a 4 × 4 grid and the flags of its even transversals.
Verification

How this was checked

Two derivations

Each step of the nonexistence proofs was derived twice, by separate sessions or separate programs, or audited with independent code. Each paper states exactly which parts rest on one implementation.

Controls

The same machinery was run on known graphs of the same shape. It must let them through; it does. The search engines recovered known graphs as must-find controls. The uniform condition accepts every existing parameter set of the table with integral eigenvalues up to 1300 vertices.

Referees

The papers were reviewed by separate AI sessions (Claude, including a different model, Claude Fable, and Codex) acting as auditors or referees; for 288, 405, 476 and the two-graphs the referee re-implemented every computation from the text.

Open data

Each paper is on Zenodo and says where its code, logs and data are archived. Every new graph is in graph6 format with a short verifier that checks A² = kI + λA + μ(J − I − A) exactly; see New graphs.
