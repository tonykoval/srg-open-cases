A strongly regular graph with parameters (288, 112, 36, 48)

On 1 November 2026 Brouwer's table marked srg(288,112,36,48) and its complement srg(288,175,110,100) with “?”: no graph was known. The row's comment is “pg(7,15,3)?”, because these are the parameters of the point graph of a hypothetical partial geometry pg(7,15,3). We construct one graph Γ, and hence its complement. Γ has 64 + 224 vertices: the points of 𝔽₈², and the 224 cosets of a cyclic subgroup of order 12 in the affine group K = 2⁶:(7:6) of order 2688, the primitive group PrimitiveGroup(64,7) of the GAP library. K is the full automorphism group and has two vertex orbits, so Γ is neither vertex-transitive nor a Cayley graph. Γ has only 64 cliques of size 8, so it is not geometric, and the existence of pg(7,15,3) remains open.

A pg(s,t,α) with s = 7, t = 15, α = 3 has v = (s+1)(st+α)/α = 288 points, k = s(t+1) = 112, λ = s − 1 + t(α − 1) = 36 and μ = α(t+1) = 48. No reference is given in the row. Nelson and Swartz (arXiv:2507.23039, Appendix B) show by computer that no Cayley graph on a group of order 288 has these parameters; Γ is not a Cayley graph, in agreement with this.

Construction

Points of 𝔽₈², and 24-sets moved by an affine group

Let 𝔽₈ = 𝔽₂(ω) with ω³ = ω + 1, let Tr(z) = z + z² + z⁴ be the trace to 𝔽₂, and put V = 𝔽₈² with the quadratic form Q(x, y) = Tr(xy). Seen as a 6-dimensional space over 𝔽₂, V carries a non-degenerate form Q of plus type, with 35 non-zero singular and 28 non-singular vectors.

K is the group of the 2688 maps (x, y) ↦ σe(a x2j, a−1 y2j) + b, with σ(x, y) = (y, x), e ∈ {0, 1}, a ∈ 𝔽₈*, j ∈ {0, 1, 2} and b ∈ V. It is generated by the translations, the maps (x, y) ↦ (ax, a−1y), the coordinate swap σ and the Frobenius map. The stabiliser of 0 is the Frobenius group 7:6 of order 42, K acts primitively on V, and GAP identifies it as PrimitiveGroup(64,7). Every element of K preserves Q up to translation.

Let C = ⟨γ⟩ with γ(x, y) = (y² + 1, x²), a cyclic subgroup of order 12 and index 224 in K. Let T ⊂ V be the 24 points (θ, θ), (θ, θ+1), (θ+1, θ), (θ+1, θ+1), (θ, θ²), (θ², θ+1), (θ+1, θ²+1), (θ²+1, θ) for θ ∈ {ω, ω², ω⁴}: the union of the C-orbits of (ω, ω) and (ω, ω²). Its stabiliser in K is exactly C. Finally let R be the ten maps ri(x, y) = (aix + bi, ai−1y + b′i) of the table below (Table 1 of the paper).

The vertex set of Γ is V together with the 224 left cosets gC (g ∈ K). Two points u, w of V are adjacent when Q(u + w) = 1; a coset gC and a point u are adjacent when g−1(u) ∈ T; two cosets gC and hC are adjacent when g−1h ∈ CRC. The set CRC has 1056 elements and is closed under inversion. Equivalently, a coset gC is the 24-set g(T) ⊂ V, a point is adjacent to the 24-sets that contain it, and g(T) is adjacent to the 88 sets gcri(T) with c ∈ C. The partition into the two orbits is equitable: a point has 28 neighbours in V and 84 cosets, a coset 24 points and 88 cosets.

Strong regularity, A² = 112I + 36A + 48(J − I − A), is a computer result; there is no hand proof yet. Two construction programs written separately, in standard-library Python and in GAP, build Γ from this definition and count the common neighbours of all pairs: 36 for every edge, 48 for every non-edge.

Uniqueness, in a narrow sense

The only graph for this group with these two orbits

K has exactly six faithful two-orbit actions on 288 points (orbit lengths 224 + 64) and no transitive one. An exact model returned no graph for five of them, and exactly two solutions, both isomorphic to Γ, for the action on the cosets of C₁₂. An independent enumeration confirmed five of the six verdicts; for one action, with stabiliser A₄, it was not finished, and that verdict rests on one program. So, up to isomorphism, Γ is the only srg(288,112,36,48) on which PrimitiveGroup(64,7) acts with two orbits. This is not a classification of the parameter set: how many graphs exist, and whether one of them is geometric, is open.

How it was found

A two-orbit search over coset actions

The graph was found on 1 November 2026 by an extension of the two-orbit search that gave the graphs on 160, 232, 261 and 280 vertices. For a group G and two subgroups H₁, H₂, a G-invariant graph on G/H₁ ⊔ G/H₂ is a union of symmetrised orbitals. With one Boolean variable per orbital class, strong regularity becomes a finite system of quadratic equations, solved exactly by CP-SAT; every solution is re-checked by computing A² in integers. The sweep covered 71 groups, the primitive groups of degree 33 to 64 and the transitive groups of degree 33 to 35 from the GAP libraries: 406,273 coset actions against the open rows of Brouwer's table, on three 24-core servers. Each server first ran a positive control (a known srg(160,54,18,18) action had to be found with all its solutions) and a planted negative; both passed on every server. The hit was the action of PrimitiveGroup(64,7) on the cosets of C₁₂ and of the point stabiliser 7:6, with 27 orbital classes. The search is how Γ was found, not part of its proof.

Verification

Independent checks

The graph was checked by programs that share no code: two independent exact checks of A² = 112I + 36A + 48(J − I − A), one of them a standalone script written for this hit that also computes the spectrum and the group; the 30-line standard-library verifier below; and a separate verification session with its own code. Two canonical-labelling programs, pynauty and dreadnaut, give the same canonical form for the search graph and for the graphs rebuilt from the definition by the Python and GAP constructions. A blind referee rebuilt Γ from the text of the definition alone and obtained the same canonical certificate. The verifier on this site's copy of the file:
