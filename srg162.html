Strongly regular graphs · open case from Brouwer's table

There is no strongly regular graph with parameters (162, 69, 36, 24)

No graph on 162 vertices, each of degree 69, where adjacent vertices share 36 neighbours and non-adjacent vertices share 24. The proof uses the lattice method of the papers on 69 and 99 vertices, now with gluing into dimension 26, where it needs Chenevier's classification of unimodular lattices. No symmetry of the graph is assumed.

Step 1 · spectrum

From eigenvalues to vectors

The adjacency matrix A has three eigenvalues. The matrix G = A + 3I + 5J sends the eigenvalue −3 to 0, so G is positive semidefinite of rank 24 and is the Gram matrix of 162 vectors in 24 dimensions.

eigenvalue of A69multiplicity 1  → G: 882
eigenvalue of A15multiplicity 23  → G: 18
eigenvalue of A−3multiplicity 138  → G: 0

Each vertex becomes a vector of norm 8; adjacent vertices have inner product 6, non-adjacent ones 5. They span an even lattice L of rank 24. For a maximal even overlattice N, the vector y = s/126 (s the sum of the vertices) has norm 9, pairs 7 with every vertex, and lies in N# but not in N, while 2y ∈ N.

Controls

The same programs on graphs that exist

The controls are known graphs whose representation has the same mechanism: y₀ of order 2 with q(y₀) = 1 and a 3-part ℤ/3, so that N′ has determinant 3 and glues with A₂ or ⟨3⟩. Among the representations scanned, no known graph on at most 300 vertices has exactly form A or B, so the controls test the mechanism and the code paths, not the target shape. Truth-side runs build the true lattice; blind runs search all unimodular lattices of the right dimension for the true host with the production code.

No truth-side control exercises the forced step (audit reservation F2).

Verification

How each step was checked

The result was obtained in a single working session on 27 September 2026, then audited independently by a separate agent with its own code. The audit's verdict: "pass, with reservations, none of which touches a verdict". A standalone plain-Python checker, using no PARI, Hecke or campaign code, re-checked all hosts and certificates afterwards.

Two defects, found and fixed before the final runs. An appending write that could leave a stale input file, and a basis change with very large entries that made the floating-point bounds lose vectors, affected the early versions. A consistency check exposed both; every earlier result was discarded, the count certificate was made part of every production run, and the audit's rerun of the check agreed on 30 of 30 hosts.

The code, computations, controls, audit, referee reports and standalone checker were produced by Claude agents working under the author's direction, each in a separate session. No step rests on the judgement of an AI system: every claim is proved in the text or reduced to a named computation that can be rerun. The author is responsible for all content. The paper has not yet been reviewed by a human referee.
