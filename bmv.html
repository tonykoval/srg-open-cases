Strongly regular graphs · a uniform condition on the parameters

The Bannai–Munemasa–Venkov lattice method for strongly regular graphs

A uniform discriminant-form condition and new nonexistence results. The lattice method of Bannai, Munemasa and Venkov, made to work for every strongly regular graph with integral eigenvalues, gives a test on (v, k, λ, μ) alone. Seventy complementary pairs that Brouwer's table lists as open fail it, so no graph exists for any of them. Its simplest case rules out an infinite family of negative-Latin-square parameters. No symmetry of the graph is assumed.

The method is not new

Bannai, Munemasa and Venkov (2005) proved the nonexistence of certain tight spherical designs by studying the lattice the design spans: the design identities bound its discriminant group, an even sublattice or overlattice is formed, and the Milgram–Braun formula for Gauss sums excludes what is left. Nebe and Venkov (2013) refined it with Sylow-wise anisotropy and the Gauss sums of the Sylow subgroups and excluded infinitely many dimensions. Tight 4-designs are strongly regular graphs, as Bannai, Munemasa and Venkov note; their dimension-46 case is srg(1127,486,165,243).

What the paper adds, as far as a literature search on 9 October 2026 could tell (it did not include forward citation searches from these two papers or the Russian-language literature):

New, as far as we found

  • A dual-vector identity and a distinguished class ȳ for any integral representation b(A − ρI) + cJ of any strongly regular graph with integral eigenvalues. This turns the first, genus-level step of the method into one condition on the parameters.
  • Its simplest case, the textbook congruence for even lattices of odd determinant applied to the centred representation, and the resulting exclusion of the NLm(q) family below.
  • A complete sweep of Brouwer's table up to 1300 vertices, with the 70 exclusions of Table 1, each graded.

Not new

  • The method itself (Bannai–Munemasa–Venkov, Nebe–Venkov).
  • The step “maximal integral overlattice ⇒ anisotropic discriminant form”, used by Akiyama for srg(266,45,0,9).
  • The facts about finite quadratic forms (Nikulin, Conway–Sloane, Milnor–Husemoller) and the parity congruence for even lattices of odd determinant (Conway–Sloane, Elkies).
In plain words

What the condition says

The adjacency matrix A of a strongly regular graph has three eigenvalues, fixed by the parameters. A suitable integer combination G = b(A − ρI) + cJ kills one eigenspace, so G is positive semidefinite and is the Gram matrix of v vectors, one per vertex, with integer inner products. They span an integral lattice L, the vertex lattice. If the diagonal of G is even, L is even, and it has a largest even overlattice M.

M leaves a finite remainder, the discriminant form D(M) = M#/M, a finite abelian group with a quadratic form. Three things constrain it. Because M is maximal, D(M) has no isotropic element. Milgram's formula fixes the phase of its Gauss sum by the rank. And an exact identity for dual vectors, which holds because the vertices satisfy the strongly-regular equations, ties every element of D(M) to the class of the rescaled sum of all vertex vectors. Finite quadratic modules without isotropic elements are few and small, so one can list them all. If none satisfies everything, the graph cannot exist.

The test needs only (v, k, λ, μ): no theta series, no gluing into unimodular lattices, no search over graphs. It is weaker than the full method of the single-set papers on this site. A parameter set that passes is not thereby shown to exist.

The simplest case · Corollary 4.1

The parity corollary

Let srg(v, k, λ, μ) have integral restricted eigenvalues, ρ ∈ {r, s}, θ the other one, n the multiplicity of θ. Put

a = v / gcd(v, k − ρ), b = a·sgn(θ − ρ), c = −b(k − ρ)/v, ν = c − bρ, ℓ = a·|θ − ρ|

If ν is even and ℓ is odd, then n is even and (−1)n/2 ≡ Δ (mod 4) for some positive integer Δ all of whose prime factors divide ℓ. In particular, if every prime factor of ℓ is ≡ 1 (mod 4), then n ≡ 0 (mod 4).

Proof: (ρ, b, c) is admissible and centred, with e = ℓ and T = 0, so the vertex lattice L is even of rank n and ℓL# ⊆ L; hence det L divides ℓn. Apply (F5) with Δ = det L. No overlattice, no Gauss sum and no computation beyond the parameters is needed. The unimodular special case of (F5), that an even unimodular lattice has rank divisible by 8, is the step by which Bannai–Munemasa–Venkov and Nebe–Venkov exclude tight designs.

An infinite family · Corollary 4.2

Negative-Latin-square parameters

Corollary 4.2. Let q = pe with p ≡ 1 (mod 4) prime, and let m be odd with 1 ≤ m ≤ q − 2. There is no strongly regular graph with the parameters of NLm(q),

( q², m(q + 1), −q + m² + 3m, m(m + 1) )

Proof sketch: the restricted eigenvalues are r = m and s = m − q, with f = (q + 1)(q − 1 − m). The complement is NLq−1−m(q), again with odd index, so one may assume p ∤ m + 1. With ρ = s the corollary above gives a = q, G = q(A − (m − q)I) − (m + 1)J, ν even and ℓ = q², whose only prime is p ≡ 1 (mod 4). But n = f = (q + 1)(q − 1 − m) ≡ 2 (mod 4), since q + 1 ≡ 2 (mod 4) and q − 1 − m is odd.

It contains the known nonexistence of srg(289, 54, 1, 12) = NL3(17), proved by Bondarenko and Radchenko (2013) by a different route; here it is a three-line second proof. It agrees with the known graphs: Paley(q²) has the parameters of NL(q−1)/2(q) with (q − 1)/2 even, and Latin-square parameters LSm(q) have n = m(q − 1) ≡ 0 (mod 4). Limits

What the condition does not do

limitdetail
The genus is not a function of the parametersW(3) and Q(4,3) are both srg(40,12,2,4). In the same representation A + 4I the blind audit found D(M) ≅ ⟨½⟩ for W(3) and a group of order 72 for Q(4,3). The test therefore only asks whether some admissible module exists; any argument that pins the genus from the parameters alone would be unsound.
Finitely many representationsA row that passes every tested representation is not shown to pass the condition. Only the smallest admissible c is tested on each side.
Scan coverageThe V1 sweep of all 1402 open pairs with integral eigenvalues is complete (2804 representations; 227 timeouts at 90 s rerun at 900 s): 70 excluded, 1318 admit a module on both sides, 14 admit one on one side and are undecided on the other after 900 s.
StrengthGenus-level only. Where admissible modules exist, for example srg(88,27,6,9) and srg(99,14,1,2), it says nothing.
Verification

How it was checked, and the grade

A blind adversarial audit by a separate Claude Fable session re-derived the obstruction from first principles before reading the derivation, wrote the decider V3, computed the actual discriminant forms of 254 real graphs and found the W(3)/Q(4,3) example. Codex (OpenAI) independently reviewed the uniform lemma and the two main hand proofs, found a missing step in Lemma 2.4(3), supplied its repair, and wrote V4. A further Fable session made the literature check. No step rests on the judgement of an AI system: every claim is proved in the text or is an exact finite computation that can be rerun.

The grade is stated per row. Hand proofs for the 28 H rows: 20 by the parity corollary, which needs only the arithmetic in the reason column, and 8 by the phase arguments above. Computer-assisted for the 42 C2 rows: exhaustive finite enumerations of quadratic modules, each repeated by at least two independent programs; no row rests on one program only.
