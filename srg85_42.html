Strongly regular graphs · settled by existence

srg(85, 42, 20, 21) exists

The conference-type parameter set (85,42,20,21) was marked “?” in Brouwer's table. In October 2026 Harshit Verma published a symmetric conference matrix of order 86, arXiv:2610.08893v1, and this settles the row: the graph exists. The result and the construction are entirely his. We recomputed his matrix independently and confirmed every finite claim we checked. Before his paper we had proved several restrictions on the automorphisms of such a graph; his graphs satisfy all of them. Download the 86 derived graphs and 10 representatives.

The result

Verma's conference matrix of order 86

Verma gives a 12 × 12 array of integers M and a sign vector t. They describe a matrix with one row and column for each of two points ∞ and ∞′ and for each of the 84 points (i, a), i = 0…11, a ∈ ℤ₇; the bits of Mij give the signs of a circulant 7 × 7 block. The result is a symmetric conference matrix C of order 86: symmetric, zero diagonal, ±1 elsewhere, and C² = 85I. Hence there is a regular two-graph on 86 points, and each of its 86 descendants is an srg(85,42,20,21). Verma proves that the descendants fall into exactly ten isomorphism classes, one for each orbit of the group G = ⟨g, h⟩ that he gives (orbits {∞}, {∞′}, six orbits of size 7 and two of size 21).

Our role

Independent confirmation

We read M and t from the TeX source of v1, rebuilt C with our own code in exact integer arithmetic and checked the claims below. We checked only the finite claims. The search that found the matrix is not described in v1, and the proof does not need it.

We also computed the full automorphism group of every descendant. It is the stabiliser of the root in G: order 21 for the roots ∞ and ∞′, order 3 for the six orbits of size 7, trivial for the two orbits of size 21. The group of order 21 is the Frobenius group 7:3 (hgh⁻¹ = g²), not ℤ₂₁. The complements of the ten graphs are again srg(85,42,20,21), descendants of the two-graph of −C, and none of them is isomorphic to one of the ten; so the matrix gives twenty graphs.

The file name of our report, docs/verma86_verification_2026_10_12.md, refers to the order 86 of the conference matrix; the graphs have 85 vertices.

Before the existence result

What we had shown about automorphisms

While the row was open, we studied which automorphisms an srg(85,42,20,21) could have. Now that graphs exist, these statements restrict their symmetries. Proved means a hand argument; computational means an exhaustive computation with controls.

No conflict. Every group order that occurs in the twenty graphs (1, 3, 21) is allowed, and every exclusion holds for all of them. The order-7 element of Γ∞ and of Γ∞′ gives two quotient matrices; both appear in our complete list of 1,536 ℤ₇-quotients with one fixed point, which reproduces the count of Behbahani and Lam. This was the first must-find control of that list on a real graph.

The classification remains open. For example, it is unknown whether a graph with an automorphism of order 5, or of order 3 with f = 1, 13 or 19, exists. These are questions about the class, not about existence.
