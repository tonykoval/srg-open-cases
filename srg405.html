Strongly regular graphs · open case from Brouwer's table

There is no strongly regular graph with parameters (405, 132, 63, 33)

No graph on 405 vertices, each of degree 132, where adjacent vertices share 63 neighbours and non-adjacent vertices share 33. Its complement would be the point graph of a partial geometry pg(8,33,6), so that geometry does not exist either. The vertices become vectors of an even lattice of rank 31. A new, elementary counting lemma bounds its roots from above; theta series of a glued unimodular lattice force an identity that the lemma cannot satisfy. No symmetry of the graph is assumed.

Step 1 · spectrum

From eigenvalues to vectors

The adjacency matrix A has eigenvalues 132, 33 and −3. The matrix G = A + 3I + J sends the eigenvalue −3 to 0, so G is positive semidefinite of rank 31 and is the Gram matrix of 405 vectors in 31 dimensions.

eigenvalue of A132multiplicity 1  → G: 540
eigenvalue of A33multiplicity 30  → G: 36
eigenvalue of A−3multiplicity 374  → G: 0

Each vertex becomes a vector of norm 4; adjacent vertices have inner product 2, non-adjacent ones 1. They span an even lattice L of rank 31, and we fix a maximal even overlattice N. The normalized sum y = s/270 has norm 3 and inner product 2 with every vertex; it lies in N# but not in N, and 2y ∈ N.

Step 4 · the counting lemma, worked

Why a norm-4 vector meets at most three vertices in 3

Let w ∈ S₄, so w² = 4 and ⟨w, y⟩ = 2, and let zᵢ = ⟨w, vᵢ⟩. The inequality of step 2 with a = 1 is an exact budget:

Σ (zᵢ − 1)(zᵢ − 2) = 36·4 + 168·4 − 3·270·2 + 405·2 = 144 + 672 − 1620 + 810 = 6

Every term is a nonnegative even integer. Each value of zᵢ costs:

z ≤ −2≥ 12
z = −16
z = 02
z = 10
z = 20
z = 32
z = 46
z ≥ 5≥ 12

With a budget of 6, at most three entries can equal 3 (and z = 4 happens only when w is a vertex vector). Every pair (root r, vertex i) with ⟨r, vᵢ⟩ = 1 gives such an entry 3, for w = vᵢ − r, and every entry 3 of a non-vertex w comes from exactly one such pair. Each root has 36 of these pairs, so

36·|R₀| ≤ 3·(|S₄| − 405)

Controls for the lemma · Remark 4.3 of the paper

The lemma on graphs that exist

For any graph with smallest eigenvalue −3 represented by A + 3I + J, the same counting gives (r + 3)|R₀| ≤ 3(|S₄| − v), where r is the positive restricted eigenvalue, provided every root is orthogonal to y. Six real graphs, with a maximal even overlattice computed exactly and all vectors of norm ≤ 4 listed:

On the line graph of PG(4,2) every non-vertex w ∈ S₄ has exactly three entries 3 (140 such w), so the bound is attained. The step “every root is orthogonal to y” is specific to the parameters: for K₃,₃,₃,₃ = srg(12,9,6,9) it fails, and so does the inequality (720 > 684). Maximal even overlattices are not unique; the referee's own choices gave different numbers for GQ(2,2) and PG(3,2), which also satisfy the lemma.

A control caught a real bug. An early version of the audit's row builder dropped some constant terms and produced a false exclusion of g₃ that needed no lemma. The PG(4,2) control could not see it, because ⟨10⟩ has no short vectors; the D₇ toy, which has the same glue data as g₃, did. The paper states the lesson: every row builder needs a planted control whose glue lattice has short vectors.

The derivation was done by Claude Opus agents. The blind audit was done by Claude Fable, a different model of the same vendor, with its own code and no access to the derivation's code. A paper-side recomputation rebuilt the g₃ rows a third time from the formulas alone. An independent referee (a fresh Claude Fable session, own PARI/GP and Python code, nothing imported) re-implemented every computational part and recommended acceptance with minor revisions, with no fatal or major finding. A Codex (OpenAI) review then re-checked the printed equations with its own script and passed them; its remarks are applied in the published version. The grade is computer-assisted: the main form g₃ can be checked with pencil and paper, while the type lists and certificates for g₂, g₀ and g₁ are computer outputs. A human check of the counting lemma, which is new and load-bearing, is still owed; no human expert has read the paper yet.
