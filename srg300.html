Strongly regular graphs · open case from Brouwer's table

There is no strongly regular graph with parameters (300, 117, 60, 36)

No graph on 300 vertices, each of degree 117, where adjacent vertices share 60 neighbours and non-adjacent vertices share 36; hence none with the complementary parameters (300,182,100,126) either. The vertices become vectors of an even lattice of rank 27. By hand, a maximal even overlattice has one of exactly eight discriminant forms. Gluing each to an explicit lattice of rank 5 gives an even unimodular lattice of rank 32, whose harmonic theta series give exact linear equations in the numbers of short dual vectors; with the vectors that the graph itself supplies, exact Farkas certificates show that none of the eight systems has a solution. No symmetry of the graph is assumed.

The row

The parameter set in Brouwer's table

The parameters (300,117,60,36) satisfy the integrality conditions, the absolute bound 300 ≤ f(f+3)/2 = 377 and both Krein conditions, so they are feasible in the usual sense. When the paper was deposited on 8 November 2026, the row of Brouwer's table for v = 251–300 was marked “?” (existence unknown), with spectrum 27²⁶, (−3)²⁷³ and no comment. The table will show “?” until Brouwer updates it; this page and the paper are a preprint, not a change of the table.

The matrix G = A + 3I + J sends the eigenvalue −3 to 0, so it is positive semidefinite of rank 1 + 26 = 27, with diagonal 4 and off-diagonal entries 2 (adjacent) and 1 (non-adjacent). It is the Gram matrix of 300 vectors of norm 4 in ℝ²⁷. The complement srg(300,182,100,126) has spectrum 182¹, 2²⁷³, (−28)²⁶. Library entry for the row: SRG Library, srg(300,117,60,36).

Context

Smallest eigenvalue −3: Koolen and Gebremichel's Table 1

By Neumaier's theorem (1979), apart from the Latin-square type (μ = 6) and the Steiner type (μ = 9), only finitely many parameter sets can be realised by primitive strongly regular graphs with smallest eigenvalue −3. Koolen and Gebremichel (Electron. J. Combin. 29(2), 2022, Conjecture 1.2) conjectured that such a graph has μ ∈ {6, 9} or v ≤ 276, and listed in their Table 1 twelve parameter sets with v > 276 and μ ∉ {6, 9} that were open at the time. (300,117,60,36) is one of them. The paper records how, to the author's knowledge, the other eleven were excluded:

The proofs of Liao and Yu and of Liao, Wang, Yu and Cai were not re-checked for the paper. With this result all twelve parameter sets of Koolen and Gebremichel's Table 1 are excluded. As the paper says, this is evidence for the conjecture, not a proof of it, and it says nothing about open parameter sets with v ≤ 276.

Controls

The same machinery lets real graphs through

The method was run on graphs that exist and have the same kind of representation, through the generators used for the target, with the true counts of an exact census of their dual vectors planted into the equations. In every run all residuals are exact zeros, no true vector type is pinned and no lower bound is violated; where a mutation was planted, it was detected. A control cannot prove the theorem; it shows that the mechanism lets existing configurations through.

What the controls cannot test: no graph with these parameters exists, so the classification of the forms cannot be tested on the object itself; planted truth at the shell of norm 6 exists only for AG(3,3); and the infeasible branch itself is reached by no existing graph. That is what the exact certificates and the hand proofs are for.

The verifiers were AI systems working under the author's direction, each with its own code. A blind referee from a different model family (Claude Fable) re-derived the set-up before opening the proposer's files, wrote its own generator and found identical unknowns, lower bounds and row spaces; with its own generator it obtained certificates for five of the forms and the three solver-free contradictions. Codex (OpenAI) verified the classification step by hand and checked the form g₄ and an AG(3,3) control with its own code. JetBrains Junie, a third system, wrote the independent generator, its certificates for all eight forms, its own pin decider, its controls and its check of the class of w. The independence achieved is independence of session, of code and, for Codex and Junie, of vendor.

The grade is computer-assisted: the proof depends on computers for the admissibility of vector types (the pins) and for the infeasibility of the eight systems. The paper is a preprint and has not yet been refereed by an independent human reader. The steps that were not written out by hand for any model, and the inputs taken from the literature (Milgram's formula, gluing, the modularity of harmonic theta series), are listed in Section 7 of the paper.

Rerun it

How to check the result

The Zenodo record holds the paper, its LaTeX source and the data archive: the models and certificates of both generators, the pin decisions, the controls, the code, a SHA-256 manifest of every file and the Lean 4 project. One script with no dependencies beyond the Python standard library (Python 3.8 or later) re-checks the certificates, the pins and the controls. From the top directory of the archive:

python3 -I verify_all.py # full run, about 9 minutes on one core, about 1.3 GB python3 -I verify_all.py --quick # about 4 minutes; skips the paper-number checks and the two g7 certificates

The last line must read ALL 51 CHECKS PASSED (full run, … s). The script checks every SHA-256 of the manifest; runs the 125 exact paper-side checks (parameters, the identities, the exhaustive classification 16 → 8 forms, the class of w, the eight complements and anti-isometries, q-expansions and relation counts, harmonicity, elementary pins); re-checks the 13 + 12 Farkas certificates of the two generators and rejects their sign-reversed multipliers; re-decides every pin of the independent models (779 pairs) with its own solver-free decider, after checking that the decider admits the eleven families of forced vectors and agrees with the proposer's decider on all 2,876 cached pairs; re-checks the controls on existing graphs; and checks that the Codex rows for g₄ span the same space over ℚ as the proposer's. It does not regenerate the linear systems from scratch, which takes hours; the generators are in the archive for provenance. Integrity alone: sha256sum -c MANIFEST_SHA256.txt.

Formal verification

The finite core is checked by the Lean 4 kernel

The archive contains a Lean 4 project (Lean v4.35.0-rc4, Mathlib pinned at commit 021ce68b) in the directory lean/. Every finite computation is a decide +kernel proof evaluated by the Lean kernel, so the compiler is not trusted; the sources contain no sorry, admit, native_decide or axiom declaration, and the only axioms reported are propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound.

“There is no srg(300,117,60,36)” is not a Lean theorem: Lean confirms the finite statements into which the proof reduces it. Mutation tests (wrong signs, constants, coefficients, bounds, complements, slices) are rejected by the kernel. Section 7.9 of the paper and lean/GAPS.md in the archive list the trust base item by item; lean/HOW_TO_CHECK.md gives the build steps. The recorded builds took about an hour for the core layer on two cores (up to about 11 GB of memory) and about two and a half hours more for the extension layers, one module at a time; the Lean build is separate from verify_all.py.
