A strongly regular graph with parameters (833, 416, 207, 208)

On 29 October 2026 Brouwer's table marked srg(833,416,207,208) with “?”: no graph was known. The row's comment is “2-graph*?”: these are the parameters of a conference graph, so a graph is the same thing as a regular two-graph on 834 points, or a symmetric conference matrix of order 834, and none was known. We construct one, prove it by hand, and find that it is the first member of a family.

The parameters are self-complementary. 833 = 28² + 7² is a sum of two squares, so nothing excludes a conference matrix of order 834; but 834 = 17 · 7² + 1 is not of Mathon's form (q+2)q²+1, and we found no construction of this order in the literature.

Construction

Seventeen fibres Paley(49) over Paley(17), joined by line incidences

The vertex set is ℤ₁₇ × 𝔽₄₉. Each fibre {i} × 𝔽₄₉ induces the Paley graph Paley(49). The eight 𝔽₇-lines through 0 of 𝔽₄₉ = 𝔽₇[ω], ω² = 3, split into four square lines and four non-square lines; a direction is a linear form ℓ: 𝔽₄₉ → 𝔽₇ up to a scalar, and it is called positive if its kernel is a non-square line (x+y, x+3y, x+4y, x+6y) and negative otherwise (x, y, x+2y, x+5y).

The 136 edges of K₁₇ are partitioned into 34 four-cycles, each containing an odd number of edges of Paley(17), and in each cycle one bipartition class is marked positive so that every vertex is positive in four cycles and negative in four (a signed four-cycle cover; the table of 34 cycles is in the paper). For an edge {a, b} with a positive and b negative in its cycle, the vertices (a,u) and (b,v) are adjacent iff ℓa(u) − ℓb(v) ∈ Tab, where ℓa is one of the four positive directions (each used once at a), ℓb one of the four negative directions, and Tab = {1,2,4} if a ~ b in Paley(17) and {0,3,5,6} otherwise. Every vertex has 24 + 8·21 + 8·28 = 416 neighbours.

Proof idea

One 4 × 4 block per four-cycle and per character

Let S be the Seidel matrix. The additive characters of 𝔽₄₉ split ℂ⁸³³ into the fibre-constant space, on which S acts as 7 times the Seidel matrix of Paley(17), and six 136-dimensional spaces, one per non-trivial character τ of 𝔽₇, indexed by the pairs (vertex, direction). A line incidence maps the character of direction ℓb at b to the character of direction ℓa at a, with coefficient 7·ητ, where ητ is the Gauss period of the squares of 𝔽₇; since {1,2,4} is a (7,3,1) difference set, |ητ|² = 2. On each character space S/7 is therefore block diagonal, one 4 × 4 block per four-cycle,

H_Z = [ I₂ −2η W ; −2η̄ Wᵀ −I₂ ], W = the 2 × 2 matrix of Paley(17) signs on the cycle,

and HZ² = 17 I exactly when the sign product of the cycle is −1. Together with the fibre-constant part this gives S² = 833 I − J and S𝟙 = 0, i.e. a conference graph. A 60-line standard-library program rebuilds the graph from the table of cycles and checks all pairs in 1.4 seconds.

The family

Paley(p²) over a conference graph on 2p + 3 vertices

With p ≡ 3 (mod 4) prime, Paley(p²) fibres over any conference graph on q = 2p + 3 vertices and the same line incidences along a signed four-cycle cover, the proof goes through word for word: a conference graph on p²q vertices. Two parity counts force q ≡ 1 (mod 16). Translation-invariant covers of Paley(q) are the same as partitions of the (q−1)/2 difference classes of 𝔽q into quadruples with a zero signed sum and an odd number of square classes, and such partitions exist for every prime power q ≡ 1 (mod 16): by computer below 321, and above by an octic-coset recipe with a Jacobi-sum estimate (512 N ≥ q² − 304q − 168√q − 39). Hence for every prime p ≡ 7 (mod 8) with 2p + 3 a prime power there is a conference graph on p²(2p+3) vertices: p = 7, 23, 47, 127, 167, 199, … give symmetric conference matrices of orders 834, 25922, 214274, 4145154, 9398594, …

With five incidences per direction instead of two (Paley(49) over Paley(41)), a constraint search found a ℤ₄₁-invariant assignment: a conference graph on 2009 vertices, |Aut| = 123, hence a symmetric conference matrix of order 2010. In the range of Brouwer's table the family has only the member on 833 vertices.

Relation to Mathon's construction. With one incidence per direction (q = p + 2) the construction needs no cover: a regular tournament on the base does the job, and the orders p²(p+2)+1 are exactly those of Mathon's conference matrices. In fact Mathon's Theorem 4.1 (1978) is this d = 1 case of the template: his blocks between fibres are line incidences too, but his fibre is not a Paley graph (it is the union of the lines of slope {0} ∪ Q). With Paley fibres the result is different: our d = 1 two-graph on 442 points and the one of Mathon's matrix each have 52 isomorphism classes of descendants and none in common, so there are at least two inequivalent symmetric conference matrices of order 442 (Mathon's theorem is a family; whether ours arises from another instance of it is not known). Version 1.1 of the paper wrongly described Mathon's fibres as Paley graphs and his blocks as quadratic-residue rather than line incidences; version 1.2 corrects this. The d = 1 graph is in the data.

How it was found

A template, a reduction, and a one-minute search

The template, Paley fibres over a conference two-graph joined by line-to-line incidences, is due to Jose Yip, who used Paley(9) fibres for conference graphs on 153 vertices (personal communication, October 2026). Analysed per character of the fibre, the template forces every direction to carry exactly (q−1)/(p+1) incidences; for Paley(49) over Paley(17) that is 2, which makes the direction graph a union of four-cycles. In a brainstorming task an AI system (JetBrains Junie) worked this out and reduced the existence of the graph to a signed four-cycle cover of Paley(17); a second AI session (Claude Fable), asked to audit the derivation blind, re-derived it, found a cover by a depth-first search in under a minute, built the graph and verified it. The graph was then verified by three further programs, and a referee session re-derived the proofs and reproduced the computations with its own code. The existence of covers for every q ≡ 1 (mod 16) was proposed by the same Junie session and audited blind as well.
