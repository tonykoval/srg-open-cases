Two-graphs and equiangular lines · open question from Brouwer–Van Maldeghem

The regular two-graphs on 126 and 176 points are unique

There is only one regular two-graph on 126 points, Taylor's unitary two-graph for U₃(5), and only one on 176 points, the Higman–Sims two-graph. Equivalently, 126 equiangular lines in ℝ²¹, or 176 in ℝ²², are unique up to isometry. As a consequence, four parameter sets of Brouwer's table each have exactly one strongly regular graph. The proof is lattice-theoretic and ends in an exhaustive enumeration inside the Leech lattice.

The theorem

Two maximal line systems, each unique

126 points · ℝ²¹Seidel spectrum 25²¹ (−5)¹⁰⁵ · angle arccos 1/5Up to isomorphism there is exactly one regular two-graph on 126 points: Taylor's unitary two-graph on the 126 isotropic points of a Hermitian form over 𝔽₂₅, on which U₃(5) acts 2-transitively. Equivalently, any two sets of 126 equiangular lines in ℝ²¹ with angle arccos 1/5 are isometric.
176 points · ℝ²²Seidel spectrum 35²² (−5)¹⁵⁴ · angle arccos 1/5Up to isomorphism there is exactly one regular two-graph on 176 points: the two-graph of the Higman–Sims group. Equivalently, any two sets of 176 equiangular lines in ℝ²² with angle arccos 1/5 are isometric.

A two-graph is a switching class of graphs; it is regular when its Seidel matrix S has two eigenvalues. Regular two-graphs are exactly the equiangular line systems that meet the relative bound of Lemmens and Seidel. For angle arccos α and d < α⁻² that bound is n ≤ d(1 − α²)/(1 − dα²); for α = 1/5:

dimension dbound nsystemuniqueness
2121·(24/25)/(4/25) = 126Taylor's two-graph, U₃(5)this paper
2222·(24/25)/(3/25) = 176Higman–Sims two-graphthis paper
2323·(24/25)/(2/25) = 276the 276-line system, Co₃Goethals and Seidel, 1975

By Lemmens and Seidel, 126 and 176 are the largest numbers of equiangular lines in ℝ²¹ and ℝ²² for any angle, and every system of that size has angle arccos 1/5. So the theorem says that the maximum equiangular line systems in dimensions 21 and 22 are unique. The first two systems are both sections of the 276-line system, but the proof does not use the uniqueness of the 276-point two-graph.

Corollary

Four parameter sets with exactly one graph

A graph is a descendant of a two-graph if it arises by switching one point to be isolated and deleting it. By Seidel's theorem, every srg(v, k, λ, μ) with k = 2μ is a descendant of a regular two-graph on v + 1 = 3k − 2λ points. The automorphism groups of the two two-graphs are 2-transitive, so all descendants are isomorphic, and uniqueness passes to the graphs:

srg(175, 72, 20, 36)72 = 2·36Higman–Sims, 176 pointsHaemers' graph on the edges of the Hoffman–Singleton graphexactly 1
srg(175, 102, 65, 51)102 = 2·51its complementcomplement of Haemers' graphexactly 1

As a check that does not use the transitivity of the groups, all 126 resp. 176 descendants of each two-graph and of its complement were computed: in each of the four families they all have the same nauty canonical form. A partial geometry pg(4,12,2), whose existence Brouwer's table lists as open, would have an srg(125,52,15,26) as point graph; by the corollary its lines would have to partition the edges of Godsil's graph into 5-cliques. The paper does not pursue this.

Switching classes

One two-graph, many regular graphs

Uniqueness of the two-graph does not make the regular graphs in its switching class unique. Every srg(126,60,33,24), srg(126,65,28,39), srg(176,85,48,34) or srg(176,90,38,54) lies in the switching class of a regular two-graph on 126 resp. 176 points, so by the theorem it is a switching of the unique two-graph or of its complement. These classes contain many such graphs:

parametersgraphs found by switchinghow
srg(126, 60, 33, 24)≥ 150exact 0/1 model for switching sets that produce a regular graph, sampled with a constraint solver; all with trivial automorphism group
srg(176, 85, 48, 34)≥ 4,335100 by the same model (trivial automorphism group), plus 4,235 by Godsil–McKay switching on sets of size 6 applied to one of them

Every graph was verified exactly and distinguished by nauty canonical forms. The counts are lower bounds; the exact numbers are open. Nozaki observed the same phenomenon for the 276-point two-graph.

The derivation was done by Claude Opus agents. The blind audit was done by Claude Fable, a different model of the same vendor, instructed to be adversarial, with its own code and no access to the derivation's code; logs were compared only at the end. An independent referee (a fresh Claude Fable session, working from the paper alone) re-implemented the numerical content, including the Leech enumeration without any group action, found no fatal or major issue and recommended acceptance with minor revisions, which are incorporated. Codex (OpenAI) reviewed the derivation independently (review C32): it re-proved the hand lemmas and reproduced the Leech counts with its own integer code. The grade is computer-assisted: every hand step is proved in the paper, and the Leech enumerations cannot be done by hand. Isomorphism tests rest on nauty canonical labelling.
