Strongly regular graphs · open case from Brouwer's table

There is no strongly regular graph with parameters (69, 20, 7, 5)

No graph on 69 vertices, each of degree 20, where adjacent vertices share 7 neighbours and non-adjacent vertices share 5. In September 2026 this was the smallest parameter set marked open in Brouwer's tables. The proof turns the graph into 69 vectors of a lattice, lists every lattice that could hold them, and excludes each one. No automorphism of the graph is assumed.

Consequences

Two designs that cannot exist

CorollaryNo quasi-symmetric 2-(46, 16, 8) design with block intersection numbers 4 and 6.Its block graph, with blocks adjacent when they meet in 4 points, would be an srg(69, 20, 7, 5).
RemarkAn independent proof that there is no Steiner system S(2, 6, 46).Its 69 blocks meet in 0 or 1 point, so its block graph is an srg(69, 48, 32, 36), whose complement is an srg(69, 20, 7, 5). Nonexistence was first shown by computer search by Houghten, Thiel, Janssen and Lam (2001). This argument uses neither the design structure nor that search. The observation is due to A. E. Brouwer.
Step 1 · spectrum

From eigenvalues to vectors

The adjacency matrix A has three eigenvalues. The matrix G = 3I + A + J sends the eigenvalue −3 to 0, so G is positive semidefinite of rank 24 and is the Gram matrix of 69 vectors in 24 dimensions.

eigenvalue of A20multiplicity 1  → G: 92
eigenvalue of A5multiplicity 23  → G: 8
eigenvalue of A−3multiplicity 45  → G: 0

Each vertex becomes a vector of norm 4; adjacent vertices have inner product 2, non-adjacent ones 1. Their integer span LX is an even lattice of rank 24. It contains u = s/23, where s is the sum of the vertices, with |u|² = 12 and ⟨vᵢ, u⟩ = 4 for every vertex.

Controls

The same machinery finds graphs that exist

A search that returns "nothing" is only as good as its ability to find what exists. The quadric screen, both engines and engine 2's certificate test were run on known strongly regular graphs of the same Gram shape, placed in their own lattices, mostly with decoy vectors added. They had to recover the graph.

Standalone checker. All certificates that are not SAT proofs were re-verified by one plain-Python program (565 lines, exact integer and rational arithmetic, no numerical library, no code shared with the pipeline). It does not establish completeness of the PARI shells other than the norm-4 shells, of the candidate enumeration or of the lattice constructions, and it does not re-check the DRAT and CP-SAT verdicts.

Independent Niemeier re-implementation (6 October 2026). A separate Claude session rebuilt the 23 lattices from their glue codes using only the text of the paper, listed all 37,605 dominant candidates with a completeness certificate (orbit sizes summed against an independent count), and applied screens R and Q with exact certificates. Every row of the table above was reproduced. Its brief contained the totals, so it was not blind to the expected outcome; its completeness certificate does not depend on them.

The code was written and the computations, audits and re-implementations were run with substantial help from AI systems (Claude); ideas from gpt-6-astra and JetBrains' Junie are credited in the paper. Every suggestion was re-derived and checked, and the author is responsible for all content. The paper has not yet been reviewed by a human referee.
