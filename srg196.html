Strongly regular graphs · open case from Brouwer's table

There is no strongly regular graph with parameters (196, 81, 42, 27)

No graph on 196 vertices, each of degree 81, where adjacent vertices share 42 neighbours and non-adjacent vertices share 27. Its complement would be the point graph of a partial geometry pg(6,18,4), so that geometry does not exist either. The vertices become vectors of an even lattice of rank 25; by hand, its maximal even overlattice has one of exactly four discriminant forms. The new ingredient is the use of odd unimodular hosts of rank 27 and 28, together with the theta series of their shadows. One form falls by hand, the other three by exact Farkas certificates. No symmetry of the graph is assumed.

Step 1 · spectrum

From eigenvalues to vectors

The restricted eigenvalues are the roots of x² − 15x − 54 = (x − 18)(x + 3). The matrix G = A + 3I + J sends the eigenvalue −3 to 0, so G is positive semidefinite of rank 25 and is the Gram matrix of 196 vectors in 25 dimensions.

eigenvalue of A81multiplicity 1  → G: 280
eigenvalue of A18multiplicity 24  → G: 21
eigenvalue of A−3multiplicity 171  → G: 0

Each vertex becomes a vector of norm 4; adjacent vertices have inner product 2, non-adjacent ones 1. They span an even lattice L of rank 25, and we fix a maximal even overlattice N; its discriminant form is anisotropic. The normalized sum y = (Σ vᵢ)/280 has norm 7/10 and inner product 1 with every vertex; it lies in N#, 10y ∈ N, and its class ȳ has order 10. The parameters pass the absolute bound (196 ≤ 324) and both Krein conditions (−171 ≤ 396, 60 ≤ 28158).

Step 5a · determinant 30, worked

Two identities and four shells

For every odd unimodular lattice U of rank 27, with Nm its vectors of norm m, cm its characteristic vectors of norm m, and Hm, Hcm the corresponding sums of the degree-2 harmonic polynomial ⟨w, x⟩² − m·x²/27 (with ¼ and η for the characteristic ones):

240N₁ − 8N₂ + 45c₃ + c₁₁ = 1728 −96H₁ + 16H₂ + 21Hc₃ + Hc₁₁ = 0

Take K = diag(5,6), one of its four anti-isometries, and x = y ± (2/5)k₁ + (1/2)k₂ ∈ U, of norm 3. Then N₁ = 0, N₂ = R (the roots of N, all orthogonal to y and to x), c₃ = 2 (the vectors ±(2y + p₀)), and c₁₁ = 4 + S (the four vectors ±k₁ ± k₂ of K and S others). Each root contributes −2/9 to H₂, each norm-3 vector 1/6 to Hc₃, the four vectors of K together 106/9 to Hc₁₁, and each of the other S vectors −1/18:

−8R + 90 + 4 + S = 1728 ⇒ S − 8R = 1634 16·(−2/9)R + 21·(1/3) + 106/9 − S/18 = 0 ⇒ S + 64R = 338 ⇒ 72R = −1296, R = −18 < 0

The identities were checked on the four lattices E₈a ⊕ ℤ27−8a, a = 0, …, 3, whose (N₁, N₂, c₃, c₁₁) are (54, 1404, 0, 0), (38, 924, 0, 0), (22, 700, 0, 2048) and (6, 732, 8, 5784). Which types occur in the shells is decided by the dual-vector lemma: 22 types are rejected, 17 by the quadratic bound alone (Table 2 of the paper lists each with its value) and 5 by an exhaustive local test. Each of the four anti-isometries gives R = −18; the audit's independent three-relation certificate for this form reduces to the same statement.

A Farkas certificate is a rational vector y with (yᵀA)v ≥ 0 for every cell after shifting by the lower bounds, and y·c′ > 0; then no nonnegative solution exists. Using a subset of the constructions discards equations and is always sound. Pins and lower bounds for determinant 1470: n(0,0,0) = 1, exactly one vector each of type y and 2y, and n(0,4,±1) ≥ 196, n(0,4,0) ≥ 15876, n(ȳ,27/10,−3/10) ≥ 196, n(−2ȳ,14/5,−2/5) ≥ 196. Two further coupled certificates for determinants 70 and 630 are valid but not load-bearing.

The derivation (Claude Opus) used two implementations of the equations that share no code. The audit (Claude Fable, a different Claude model, with its own code) was blind for determinants 30, 70 and 630, but not for 1470: its first coupled model for 1470 was feasible, and while comparing models with the derivation's second implementation it found a defect in its own direction generator; after the fix its own model is exactly infeasible. The paper states this openly. An independent referee (a fresh Claude Fable session, own code) re-derived the hand arguments, checked every certificate and every type exclusion, and recommended acceptance after minor revisions, with no fatal or major finding; its points are applied. A standard-library Python checker written for the paper rechecks everything again (399 s, plus 25 to 80 minutes to rebuild the audit's determinant-1470 model). The independence is of session and of code, not of vendor. The grade is computer-assisted: the four forms and determinant 30 can be checked by hand, while the equations and certificates for 70, 630 and 1470 are generated by computer.
